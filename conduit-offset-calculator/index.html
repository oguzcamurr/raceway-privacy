    How to bend an offset

    1. Measure the depth — how far the conduit has to move to clear the obstruction, face to face.
    2. Pick an angle. Shallow angles (10°, 22.5°) pull wire more easily and look cleaner but need more room between bends; 45° and 60° are tight but harder to pull through.
    3. Find the distance between bends: depth × multiplier (or use the calculator).
    4. Mark the conduit. Without an obstruction measurement, mark the first bend, then the second one the bend distance away. Working up to an obstruction, add the shrink so the offset lands where you need it — the calculator does this for you.
    5. Bend the first mark to the angle, then roll the conduit 180°, line up the second mark and bend the same angle. Keep both bends in the same plane — sight down the pipe to check for dog-leg.
    6. Check your first piece against the obstruction before you cut a run of them.

    About the numbers: this calculator uses the point model behind apprenticeship charts — each bend is treated as a sharp corner. Real benders have a radius and some springback, which shift the marks slightly. Check your first piece. This page uses no electrical-code data.

    Common questions

    What is the multiplier for a 30 degree offset?

    2. The exact value is 1 ÷ sin 30° = 2.000, so a 30° offset needs 2 inches between bends for every inch of offset depth.

    How much does a 30 degree offset shrink?

    About 1/4 inch per inch of offset depth. The exact value is tan 15° = 0.268 inch per inch, so a 4 inch offset shrinks about 1 1/16 inches.

    Why do field-chart multipliers differ from the calculator?

    Field charts round the numbers (1.4 instead of 1.414 for 45°, 6 instead of 5.76 for 10°). On small offsets the difference is under 1/16 inch; on deep offsets it adds up. Pick the mode your crew uses and stay consistent.

    Does this account for my bender's radius?

    No. This page uses the point model behind the apprenticeship charts, where each bend is treated as a sharp corner. Real benders have a radius, so check your first piece before cutting a run.

    How do you calculate a conduit offset?

    Multiply the offset depth by the multiplier for your bend angle (1 ÷ sin θ) to get the distance between the two bends, and multiply the depth by the shrink per inch (tan of half the angle) to get the shrink. A 6 inch offset at 30° needs 12 inches between bends and shrinks about 1 5/8 inches.

    What is the multiplier for a 45 degree offset?

    1.414 (1 ÷ sin 45°), which field charts round to 1.4. The shrink is 0.414 inch per inch of offset depth, about 3/8 inch.
