    How to bend a 90° stub-up

    1. Measure the stub height you need, from the end of the conduit to the back (outside) of the finished 90.
    2. Subtract the take-up for your bender and conduit size, and mark the conduit that far from the end.
    3. Line the mark up with the arrow on the bender and bend with steady foot pressure on the heel until the stub is plumb. Check it with a level.
    4. Measure the stub to the back of the bend. If it comes out long or short by the same amount every time, your take-up is different: use "Find your bender's take-up" above.

    Worked example

    A 12" stub in 1/2" EMT with a 5" take-up: mark at 12" − 5" = 7" from the end. An 18" stub in 3/4" EMT with a 6" take-up: mark at 12".

    Back-to-back 90s

    For a U shape, bend the first 90 as above. Hook your tape on the back of that stub, mark the back-to-back distance, line the mark up with the star (the back-of-90 mark on many benders) and bend the second 90 with the bender turned around, so both stubs point the same way.

    About the numbers: these calculators use the point model behind apprenticeship charts — each bend is treated as a sharp corner. Real benders have a radius and some springback, which shift the marks slightly. Bend and measure a test piece from scrap before you bend a run. This page uses no electrical-code data.

    Common questions

    What is take-up on a conduit bender?

    Take-up, also called deduct, is the amount you subtract from the stub height to find where to mark the conduit for a 90. It depends on the bender shoe and the conduit size, and it is usually printed on the bender.

    What is the take-up for 1/2 inch EMT?

    5 inches on common 1/2-inch EMT hand benders, such as the Greenlee 840 and Klein 51606. Common 3/4-inch EMT benders use 6 inches and 1-inch benders 8 inches. Always check the number on your own bender.

    How do I find my bender's take-up?

    Mark a scrap piece, bend a 90 with the mark on the arrow, and measure the stub from the end to the back of the bend. Take-up = measured stub − mark distance. For example, a mark at 7 inches that gives a 12-inch stub means a 5-inch take-up.

    How do I measure a stub-up?

    From the end of the conduit to the back (outside) of the 90, with the stub plumb. That is the stub height this calculator uses.

    What is the shortest stub I can bend?

    A little longer than the take-up. If the stub height is equal to or shorter than the take-up, the mark would be at or past the end of the conduit.
