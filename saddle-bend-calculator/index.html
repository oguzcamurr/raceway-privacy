    How to bend a 3-point saddle

    1. Measure the height of the obstruction (plus any clearance) and the distance from the conduit end to the center of the obstruction.
    2. Center mark: distance to the center + 3/16" for every inch of height. This is the shrink up to the center of the saddle.
    3. Outer marks: 2 1/2 × the height on each side of the center mark.
    4. Bend the center 45° on the bender's center mark (a rim notch or teardrop on many benders).
    5. Bend each outer mark 22.5° the opposite way, on the arrow, keeping all three bends in one plane. For the far outer mark, turn the conduit around so that bend forms back toward the center. Bending order differs by manufacturer: Klein's guide bends the center first; Greenlee's bends an outer mark, the center, then the other outer.

    Worked example: 3-point saddle over a 2" pipe

    The pipe is 2" high and its center is 30" from the end of the conduit. Center mark: 30" + 2 × 3/16" = 30 3/8". Outer marks: 2 × 2.5 = 5" each side, at 25 3/8" and 35 3/8". The saddle uses 3/4" of conduit (total shrink).

    How to bend a 4-point saddle

    1. Measure the height, the width (how long the conduit stays up) and the distance from the conduit end to the near side of the obstruction.
    2. Pick an angle and find the distance between bends (height × multiplier) and the shrink of one offset (height × shrink per inch).
    3. Mark 2 = distance to the near side + shrink. Mark 1 = mark 2 − distance between bends.
    4. Mark 3 = mark 2 + width. Mark 4 = mark 3 + distance between bends.
    5. Bend marks 1 and 2 as an offset up (roll the conduit 180° between them), then marks 3 and 4 as an offset back down to the original line. Sight down the conduit to keep every bend in one plane.

    Worked example: 4-point saddle over a 4" × 10" box

    Height 4", width 10", near side 40" from the end, 30° bends with field-chart numbers. Distance between bends: 4" × 2 = 8". Shrink per offset: 4" × 1/4" = 1". Marks: 33", 41", 51", 59". Total shrink: 2".

    About the numbers: these calculators use the point model behind apprenticeship charts — each bend is treated as a sharp corner. Real benders have a radius and some springback, which shift the marks slightly. Bend and measure a test piece from scrap before you bend a run. This page uses no electrical-code data.

    Common questions

    What is the multiplier for a 3-point saddle?

    For the common 45° center with 22.5° outer bends, the field rule is 2.5: the outer marks go 2 1/2 inches from the center mark for every inch of saddle height. The exact point-model value is 1 ÷ sin 22.5° = 2.613.

    How much does a 3-point saddle shrink?

    Move the center mark 3/16 inch farther from the end for every inch of saddle height (the exact value is tan 11.25° = 0.199 inch). The whole saddle uses twice that, about 3/8 inch of conduit per inch of height.

    What is a saddle bend in conduit?

    A saddle carries the conduit over an obstruction, such as a pipe crossing the run, and brings it back to its original line. A 3-point saddle uses three bends; a 4-point saddle is two offsets with a straight section over the obstruction.

    When should I use a 4-point saddle instead of a 3-point?

    When the obstruction is wide, such as a box, beam or duct, or when a 3-point saddle would be too tall or too tight to pull wire through easily.

    What is the saddle mark on a bender?

    Many hand benders have a mark for the center bend of a 3-point saddle, often a rim notch or a teardrop. Line the center mark on the conduit up with it; the outer bends go on the arrow. Markings differ by manufacturer, so check your bender's guide.
