Conduit bending basics

Multiplier

Distance between the two bends of an offset = offset depth × multiplier, where the multiplier is 1 ÷ sin θ. A 6" offset at 30° needs 12" between bends.

Shrink

An offset or saddle makes the conduit reach less far along the run. Shrink per inch of depth = (1 − cos θ) ÷ sin θ: about 1/4" per inch at 30°, so a 6" offset at 30° shrinks 1 5/8".

Take-up (deduct)

For a 90° stub-up, mark the conduit at the stub height minus the bender's take-up. It is printed on most benders and depends on the shoe and conduit size.

Point model vs. your bender

Charts and these calculators treat each bend as a sharp corner. Real benders have a radius and some springback, so bend and measure a test piece from scrap before you bend a run.

Common questions

What is shrink in conduit bending?

Shrink is the length of conduit an offset or saddle uses up: after bending, the conduit reaches less far along the run than its straight length. For an offset it is depth × (1 − cos θ) ÷ sin θ. A 6-inch offset at 30° shrinks about 1 5/8 inches.

What is a conduit bending multiplier?

The number you multiply the offset depth by to get the distance between the two bends: 1 ÷ sin θ for a bend angle θ. Common chart values are 6 for 10°, 2.6 for 22.5°, 2 for 30°, 1.4 for 45° and 1.2 for 60°.

What is take-up when bending a 90?

The amount you subtract from the stub height to find the mark for a 90° bend. Common EMT hand benders use 5 inches for 1/2-inch, 6 inches for 3/4-inch and 8 inches for 1-inch, but it varies by bender, so check the number on yours.
